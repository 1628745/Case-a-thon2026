Data has a shelf life and a source reputation.

We measured both on six months of real Chongqing deliveries. Source reputation is what matters here: a sloppy source makes promises worse in proportion to its share, from the first week its records enter training. The data's shelf life is long: months-old history stayed informative, and only a model that is never retrained decays. A three-layer Guard built on that evidence restores most of the lost accuracy, and we show where it still falls short.

The decision. A dispatcher accepts a package and must promise a delivery window. A LightGBM model gives an expected duration (median) and a promise (80th percentile). A promise that is too short is a missed promise; one that is too long is padded, uncompetitive service. The model is retrained every week on recent deliveries, so whatever enters the feed enters the promise.

5 · Countermeasure

The Guard: three layers around the model

L1 Ingest checks

Label-free flags on every record: non-positive duration; five or more packages from one courier scanned in the same minute; repeated package id (keep the first); GPS fix older than 30 min; courier more than 30 km from the stop. Flagged rows are quarantined at weight 0.05, never deleted.

L2 Training weights

weight = recency × reputation × flag. Recency halves every h weeks. Each courier earns a reputation from the training window only: their flag rate and how far their durations sit from other couriers in the same area and hour, shrunk toward neutral with a 30-record prior. No courier may hold more than 2× the median courier's weight. Stale history is kept (it stayed informative); a feature-store alert goes to the data steward when its mean age passes 7 days.

L3 Serve-time check

The promise is a conformal P80: fit on the older window weeks, calibrated per area type on the most recent week so each type stays near 80% coverage. A package is deferred to a person when its area is unseen, its GPS fix is stale, or its interval is wider than k× its area type's usual width.

Alternatives we rejected

  • Hard-delete flagged rows. Irreversible and loses rare valid data; the random-removal control shows lost volume costs accuracy.
  • Generic outlier detection. Fails when the sloppy feed becomes the majority of a courier's records.
  • Retrain on shorter windows only. Helps staleness, amplifies the sloppy feed and starves rare areas (see the shelf-life curve).
  • A bigger model. Memorizes noisy labels.
  • A drift alarm alone. Detects, does not fix. We keep one as the monitor.
  • Manual cleaning. Does not scale and cannot be tested.
6 · Stress tests and limits

Held-out weeks, an unseen defect, and where it breaks

The Guard settings were frozen (git tag guard-v1-frozen) before weeks 22-25 were ever scored. Those weeks include most of National Day Golden Week and a volume surge, and a defect the Guard was never tuned on: 15% of couriers start scanning packages delivered early (at 40-70% of the true time) from week 18.

Tradeoffs

    When not to use this system

    • When labels come from one source only: reputation needs peers to compare against.
    • When the data's problems are not visible in scans, GPS or timing (e.g. the wrong package delivered on time).
    • When nobody is staffed to handle deferrals and review quarantined sources.
    • For a new city without a few weeks of history: recency, reputation and conformal calibration all need a window.
    7 · Oversight and governance

    Who does what, and when to stop

    Roles

    RoleResponsibility
    DispatcherUses the promise; handles deferred packages (approve the promise, override it, or flag the source); logs corrections.
    Data stewardReviews quarantined records and low-reputation sources weekly; can restore a courier's reputation or escalate a scanner problem.
    Model ownerSigns off each weekly retrain (scorecard lines, monitor alerts) before it serves; owns rollback.
    Operations managerApproves the pilot, its district and its stop conditions; receives the weekly report.

    Defer rules and the correction path

    1. The Guard defers a package when its area is unseen in the training window, its GPS fix is older than 30 min, or its promise interval is unusually wide (more than k× its area type's usual width). Stale history does not defer packages; it raises a feature-store alert to the data steward.
    2. The dispatcher sees the health card and reason, then approves, overrides the promise, or flags the source.
    3. Flags and overrides go to the data steward's queue with the reason and the courier's reputation.
    4. The steward's decisions feed back as reputation adjustments at the next weekly retrain; the model owner sees the count.

    Human effort vs. compute

    Bounded pilot

    • Shadow mode, one district, 4 weeks: the Guard computes promises and deferrals alongside the current process; dispatchers see but do not act on them.
    • Checkpoints at weeks 2 and 4: promise-miss rate 17-23%, deferrals at or under 10% of packages, no area type more than 5% worse than without the Guard, steward queue cleared weekly.
    • Owner: the model owner runs it; the operations manager decides go/no-go at week 4.
    • Stop conditions: two consecutive weeks with miss rate outside 15-25%; deferrals above 15% for a week; any area type's error more than 10% worse; a steward backlog older than one week.
